---
title: "Roast level"
description: "Roasting turns a grassy green seed into coffee. Lighter roasts keep origin character; darker roasts trade it for roast flavour and bitterness."
tags:
  - coffee
---

In the roaster, sugars caramelise and the Maillard reaction builds hundreds of aroma compounds. Around 196 °C the beans audibly crack as steam escapes: _first crack_.

- **Light:** stops shortly after first crack. Bright, floral, fruity; shows [[Terroir]].
- **Medium:** sweeter, more caramel, less acidity.
- **Dark:** past _second crack_. Smoky, bittersweet, oils on the surface.

Darker beans are more porous and dissolve faster, so they need gentler brewing (cooler [[Water temperature]], coarser [[Grind size]]). Roasted coffee then starts to age, see [[Freshness]].
