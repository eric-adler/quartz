## Dissolving

Brewing is dissolving: ground coffee is about ==30% soluble==, and the water takes the flavours out in order. ==Acids== dissolve first, then sugars, and the bitter compounds come last. A good cup extracts roughly ==18–22%== of the coffee's dry mass.

A cup that tastes sour and thin is ==under-extracted==. One that tastes bitter and drying is over-extracted.

## The dials

- ==Grind size== is the strongest dial: finer grounds expose more surface, so extraction speeds up.
- Hotter water extracts faster. Most brewing happens between ==90 and 96 °C==.
- The ==brew ratio== sets strength: around 1:16 for filter coffee, about 1:2 for espresso.
- A refractometer measures strength as ==total dissolved solids==, usually 1.15–1.45% for filter.

## The beans

Roasted coffee tastes best roughly ==one to four weeks== after roasting. Fresh beans release carbon dioxide, which is why pour-over starts with a ==bloom==. Robusta has about ==twice the caffeine== of arabica.

## Espresso

A classic shot is 18 g in, ==36 g out==, in 25–30 seconds, at around ==9 bar== of pressure. When water finds a weak path through the puck, it's called ==channeling==.
